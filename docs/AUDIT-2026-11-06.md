# Audit of 2026-11-06 — end of M3 (branch `m3-audit`, a1aac1e)

Scope: the two things DRIVING asks of this audit. Every security claim in SECURITY-MODEL and the README's
checks table, against a named test, with mutation as the standard. And the trust model attacked as a
whole, across the socket, torqcli, the agent, the remote, adopt, pull, strict mode, snapshots, the
ledger's mending and first-boot setup. Read-only on `/home/viper/torq`; all work was in a detached
worktree and clones of it in the scratchpad. Nothing was run as root on the host; nothing touched
`/var/lib/torq`. No part was delegated.

What was run: `cargo test` for both crates (both green at the start); 34
mutants of the broker in `cargo test`; 23 mutants in VM tests (`socket`, `agent-run`, `strict`, `remote`,
`snapshots`, `tree`, `ssh`, `boot`), four of them batches of guards I expected to survive, sixteen guards in all; and eight attack
experiments, each a VM test rewritten in a clone. In the VM experiments rebuilds are stubbed, as the
repository's own tests stub them. `installer`, `image`, `adopt` and `trust-loop` were read, not run.

Evidence (the rewritten tests, logs, every mutant's result) was kept beside the report while its findings were open, written `evidence/` below; it is not in the repository (it reproduces the findings), and was removed once they were fixed or recorded (KNOWN-GAPS 6zl, 2026-11-09).

## Verdict

**The headline holds where it matters most: no path from `torq-agent` to root was found**, not through
a proposal, a restore, a pull, an adopted or mended ledger, a snapshot label, or the launcher, and the
launcher gives a wheel member no root either. Strict mode has no quiet way round on a running machine
that a newer command opened. The record works: rewritten and cut ledgers are alarms, and what the
anchor needs from the remote is what KNOWN-GAPS says it needs.

**But the second half of the sentence has three cracks, and one is an authorisation bypass.**

- **The broker's gate decides on a proposal's tier as it was proposed; `apply` applies it to the
  configuration as it is** (H1). A non-administrator wrote another person's package list and the
  system's, with no password, over the real socket. So did a program in the owner's shell, which is the
  case the password exists for. The ledger says `tier: system, approved_by: sam` and nothing alarms. Not
  root: the files reachable are package lists. The fix is small.
- **A non-administrator, and the agent, can silence the machine and deny its owner** (M1 to M4). The
  agent, by repeating a refused proposal, held the broker's lock for 19 checks of 20: people's changes
  failed as busy, the drift check found nothing in six runs with the config repo really changed, and
  the timer's push skipped without a record. The agent, by filling its own home, stopped break-glass
  in strict mode (`No space left on device`, `Not on the record: no shell`). A non-administrator, with
  five wrong `su`s, locked the owner out of sudo, break-glass and every commit, as often as they like.
  KNOWN-GAPS has each of these as an accident or a nuisance; each is worse than written, and three of
  the four are reachable from the account the design is built to fence.
- **"What root does here cannot outlive a rebuild" is false as written** (M5). `status` and `repair`
  compare store paths by name. What root leaves in `/etc`, `/root` or `/var/lib` stays, and `show
  system alarms` says "exactly as its history says".

**Every security claim now has a row, naming its test or saying there is none:** of 84
claims, 51 are proved, 10 are weak, 14 untested, 9 false. The weak ones have a
pattern: **numbers and negatives.** "Ten minutes", "at most 20", "30", "200", "4 MiB", "3 tries" and "ten
kept" are each unpinned: the tests read the constant, so a changed constant passes. And the guards
behind the rarest events have no test at all: a changed host key, a ledger gone from the remote, a day
without a push, `main` during a commit confirmed, a remote history rewound, no ledger no shell.

**Recommendation:** fix H1 before anything else in M4 (base required on the socket, tier compared at
apply). Fix M1 before M5: the agent must not be able to hold the broker's lock. Decide M2 and M3 as the
owner's calls they are; neither needs a fallback. Restate M5's sentence. Write the tests in the table
as the work they belong to comes up; the ones that are one line each are marked.

## Findings

### H1 — high — a person's `apply` is gated on the tier the ledger recorded, not on what it does now
- **Where:** `broker/src/serve.rs:199-206` reads tier and files from the proposal's ledger entry and
  gates on them (`socket.rs:169`). `broker/src/lib.rs:2222-2226` recomputes `files` and `tier` against
  the current HEAD and uses them, for the agent's list and the commit message, but never compares them
  with what was gated. `--base` is optional for a person (KNOWN-GAPS 6g).
- **What is wrong:** `git apply` is a function of the diff *and* of HEAD. A diff can be written whose
  effect on a file is nothing at one HEAD and something at another. Then it is proposed as the first
  and applied at the second, and the gate has seen only the first.
- **Scenario** (run, through the socket, as `sam`, not in `wheel`; rebuild stubbed):
  1. `users/dev/packages.json` and `system/packages.json` are as torqcli writes them, several lines.
  2. `sam` makes his own list the same bytes. His folder: no password.
  3. `sam` proposes, with no `--base`, one diff: delete `users/dev/packages.json`, `copy from
     users/sam/packages.json` to it, the same for `system/packages.json`, and one real hunk in his own
     file. Today the delete and the copy cancel out: the broker records `tier: user`, `files:
     [users/sam/packages.json]`, and `effect` shows his own line alone.
  4. `sam` changes a line of his own list, and applies that. No password.
  5. `sam` applies the proposal from 3. The gate reads `user` and his own file: open. The copy now
     carries his new line into both targets.
  Result: `applied`, `tier: system`, `approved_by: sam`, exit 0, no password, `users/dev/packages.json`
  and `system/packages.json` changed. `torq-broker status` afterwards: OK.
- **Reach:** the target must be text that a valid `packages.json` can match at step 2, or derive from by
  hunks with shared context, and every name must be a real package or a real build undoes the change.
  So: any person's list, and `system/packages.json`. I looked for a way to a group or a sudo rule and did
  not find one: the context lines would have to be real package names. It is not root. The same steps
  work for a program in the owner's shell, which has the uid and not the password: the human gate does
  not hold for these files (the same gate: run as `sam` only). An agent's proposal is not affected: its base is required, and refused
  when stale.
- **Also:** a person shown a base-less proposal at `request system apply` is shown the effect at the
  old HEAD, not the one it will have (`cli/src/shell.rs:303`). "A person is shown what a proposal does"
  holds for the agent's only.
- **Verified:** run end to end (`evidence/e1-socket.nix`). The git behaviour
  was first checked by hand in `evidence/p.diff`.
- **Fix:** both of these. Require `--base` on the socket for everyone, as for the agent: `serve` adds
  nothing, it refuses a `propose` without one. And in `apply_inner`, after recomputing, refuse if `tier` or
  `files` differ from the proposal's entry: a refusal, ledgered, never a re-gate that could go either
  way. A test: this scenario in `tests/socket.nix`.

### M1 — medium — any account, the agent included, can hold the broker's lock, and that silences the alarms
- **Where:** `broker/src/main.rs:336-341` pushes after every `propose`, refused ones too;
  `broker/src/lib.rs:569` holds the broker's lock across the whole network exchange. `lib.rs:1165`:
  `status` takes the same lock. `lib.rs:273-276`: when busy, `drift()` returns nothing, the sticky
  remote alarm and a broken ledger included. `modules/broker.nix:197`: exit 75 keeps the old finding and
  raises no new one. `lib.rs:569` again: a push that finds the lock taken returns `None`, and records
  nothing, so "a day without a push" never starts counting.
- **What is wrong:** three reasonings that were each true alone. The review of pull kept the agent from
  `fetch-incoming` because "it goes to the network, as root"; `propose` goes there too, after every
  request, refused or not, past the 30-an-hour cap, with no entry. 6zf says `status` makes others wait; it
  does not say what waiting does to the checks. 6b says a leaked lock makes checks silent; it does not say
  anyone can hold one on purpose.
- **Scenario** (run, `remote` VM, a healthy remote on the same virtual network): two loops as
  `torq-agent`, each sending a proposal of `evil.nix`, which is refused.
  - `torq-broker status`: busy 19 times of 20 (0 of 20 before).
  - A person's propose and apply: 6 of 8 failed with 75.
  - The config repo really changed by root, then `torq-status` six times: `/run/torq/drift` never appeared.
    With the loop stopped, it appeared at once.
  - `torq-broker push`: "no remote is set (or an operation is running)", nothing recorded.
  With the ledger at 80 MB (M4), two `status` loops as `nobody` alone gave busy 10 of 10: `status` then
  holds the lock for 5 s.
  On the real internet a push takes seconds, not tenths: this gets worse, not better.
- **What it is not:** the agent gains nothing by it but denial. Stopped, everything comes back. But
  "going around the door is never invisible" has a "never" in it, and a compromised agent running beside
  a person's root shell can keep the check silent for as long as it runs.
- **Verified:** run (`evidence/e3-remote.py`, `e4.log`).
- **Fix:**
  - The push under a lock of its own, as `fetch-incoming` has; the broker's lock not across the network.
  - No push after a command that wrote nothing to the ledger.
  - `status` reads without the broker's lock, or with a shared one; the sticky alarm, the ledger's
    chain and the record of a failed push are reported even while busy.
  - A push skipped as busy is recorded, and busy for longer than a real operation can be is itself an alarm.

### M2 — medium — the agent, or anyone with a home, can fill the disk, and then there is no root and no commit
- **Where:** `hosts/image/hardware-configuration.nix:21-23`: `/home`, `/var/lib` and `/var/lib/torq` are
  subvolumes of one btrfs, one pool of free space. `broker/src/ledger.rs:259-261`. `modules/strict.nix:38`.
  KNOWN-GAPS 6zj.
- **What is wrong:** 6zj accepts "no ledger, no shell" for a full disk, an accident, and I do not reopen
  that decision. What changed under it is ADR-038: on ext4 the ledger shared a disk with homes but had
  root's reserved blocks; on btrfs, new in M3's last item, there are none, and the ledger's room is every
  account's to take. The accepted limit is now reachable on purpose by the account the design fences.
- **Scenario** (run, `snapshots` VM with strict mode, a 700 MB data disk): as `torq-agent`, `dd` to
  `/var/lib/torq-agent` until it stops. `dev` at a terminal: `torq break-glass the disk is full`, the
  right password. Output: `Error: No space left on device (os error 28)`, `Not on the record: no shell.`,
  exit 1. The ledger's last entry is the change before.
  Commits stop the same way, at the proposal's entry (read). Snapshots hold what is deleted, so `rm` by the person who
  filled it may free less than it should. If the agent did it the owner has a way back, `torq agent run -- rm`; if
  a person did it, in strict mode, it is the console and external media.
- **Verified:** run (`evidence/e5-snapshots.nix`).
- **Fix:** room the record can always have, which is no back door: a quota group on `@home` and
  `@var-lib` that leaves `@var-lib-torq` a margin (btrfs qgroups), or a small file system of its own for
  `/var/lib/torq`, as ADR-038's layout already gives it a mount. Say in 6zj that a full disk can be made.

### M3 — medium — five wrong `su`s by anyone lock the owner out of every door, as long as they keep at it
- **Where:** `modules/security.nix:93-95`. faillock counts by the *target* account, and `su` runs for any
  caller. `modules/broker.nix:410-418` shares the count.
- **What is wrong:** the lockout was added so that a program running *as the person* cannot guess. On `su`
  it also lets any other account spend the person's five. 6zf records the decision and not this. The
  test does it, `su - sam -c …su dev` (tests/socket.nix:246), and asserts the count went up.
- **Scenario** (run): `sam`, non-administrator, runs `su dev` five times with a wrong password. `dev`
  with the right password: broker 77 `that is not dev's password`; `sudo` refused. So no system change, no
  password change, no break-glass. Ten minutes, and `sam` repeats. A reboot clears it (tmpfs), and `sam`
  repeats. Under strict mode the owner has no other way to root, by the owner's choice.
  Not reachable by the agent under `torq agent run` (no new privileges, so `su` is not set-uid there). A
  service account running as itself can. The message is also wrong: it says the password is wrong.
- **Verified:** run (`evidence/e1-socket.nix`, second subtest).
- **Fix:** count `su` failures only when the caller is the target (`pam_succeed_if` on the caller, or
  a per-service `dir=`), keeping sudo, passwd and the broker's shared; a non-owner's guess at someone
  else's `su` then falls to `su`'s own delay. Or drop `su` from `lockout`: root is locked and no
  person's `su` is needed. The broker's refusal could say "locked for now" apart from "wrong".

### M4 — medium — the agent can fill the ledger, past what adopt will take
- **Where:** `broker/src/lib.rs:147` `MAX_DIFF` 4 MiB, `lib.rs:1666-1688` (the comment says "the agent
  cannot fill the ledger"), `lib.rs:1740-1746` the diff ledgered whole, `lib.rs:747` `MAX_LEDGER` 64 MiB.
- **What is wrong:** the agent's limit is 20 waiting *at this HEAD*, each up to 4 MiB of diff, and a diff
  may carry anything before its first header: git ignores it, `effect` does not show it, the ledger keeps
  it. Any change of HEAD resets the count. N19 and 6zf have the general point; 6zg's "at most 20" reads as a
  fix.
- **Scenario** (run, `remote` VM): as `torq-agent`, on the default list (the owner's packages), proposals
  of one package padded with 3.9 MB of noise. Ledger: 1.6 KB to **71.4 MB**, in 18 proposals; a person's
  one change, then three more: 83.4 MB. Nothing in `show system proposals` looks odd.
  Then: `status` takes 5.4 s, holding the lock. **A remote ledger past 64 MiB is one adopt refuses
  (`ledger_at_most`): the machine is no longer a URL**, and the remote cannot unsee it. With a remote each
  push also writes the whole ledger as a new blob into the config repo (`repo.rs:129`), and nothing
  collects them: read, not measured.
- **Verified:** ledger sizes and `status` run (`evidence/e3-remote.py`, `e4.log`). Adopt's refusal by reading: 71.4 MB
  is past `64 << 20`.
- **Fix:** bound what is kept by what it does. For the agent, a diff no larger than a small multiple of its
  `effect` (padding refused), and a total in bytes across waiting proposals that a change of HEAD does not
  reset. N19's fix (diffs by hash, beside the ledger) closes the rest for everyone, and takes the 64 MiB
  cliff away from adopt.

### M5 — medium — "what root does cannot outlive a rebuild" is false as written, and the all-clear overclaims
- **Where:** ADR-035's closing sentence and lever 3; SECURITY-MODEL "the store is read-only and rebuilt from
  the configuration"; `broker/src/lib.rs:337-352` (`status` compares store paths by name);
  `cli/src/status.rs` ("This machine is exactly as its history says").
- **What is wrong:** a rebuild replaces what the configuration owns. It does not look at anything else,
  and it does not look *inside* a store path it already has: no `nix store verify` anywhere in the
  repository. Root can leave behind `/etc/ld.so.preload` (NixOS does not manage it), `/root/.gitconfig`
  (the broker's HOME is `/root`), anything in `/var/lib` or a home, and can remount the store and change a
  path that every rebuild then reuses, the broker's own included.
- **Scenario** (run, stub rebuild, so the switch itself is not in it): files planted in `/etc` and `/root`,
  then a change applied: both still there. Then, as root, `mount -o bind,remount,rw /nix/store`, and a line
  appended to `torq-break-glass` in the store. `torq-broker status`: `OK: the machine is as the ledger says`.
  `repair`: `nothing to repair`. `torqcli show system alarms`: `This machine is exactly as its history says.`
  `nix-store --verify-path` on it: a hash mismatch, so the check exists and is one command away.
- **Verified:** run (`evidence/e5-snapshots.nix`, `e6-snapshots.nix`). That files outside NixOS's list survive a real switch: by
  reading, as the rebuilds were stubbed.
- **Fix:** mostly the sentence. What is true: what root does cannot outlive a *reinstall from the
  repository*, data aside, and a rebuild restores what the configuration names. Reword ADR-035, SECURITY-MODEL
  and the all-clear ("as its history says, as far as the broker checks"). `repair` could add `nix store
  verify` of the running closure, cheaply, for the store's half.

### M6 — medium — the guards of the rarest events have no test
Mutation, as DRIVING asks. Each of these was removed in a clone and the check named stayed green. The
table has them claim by claim; they are here because together they are the anchor's and the door's edge
cases.
- `tests/remote.nix` passes with all of these gone at once: the host-key alarm (`lib.rs:649`), the
  ledger-gone alarm (`lib.rs:654`), the day-without-a-push alarm (`lib.rs:296`), `main` held while a commit
  confirmed waits (`lib.rs:628`), the remote still holding the commit checked last (`lib.rs:664`). Also, alone:
  the alarm stopping pushes once the remote agrees again (`lib.rs:600`).
- `tests/agent-run.nix` passes with all of these gone at once: the launcher closing the shared directory's
  descriptor (`agent-run.sh:63`), the device-and-inode check (`:59`), `--no-new-privs`, and dropping the
  capability sets. So "handed the directory, not a way to it" rests on a line no test reads: leak the
  descriptor and `openat(fd, "..")` is the home.
- `tests/socket.nix` passes with all of these gone at once: `unlock_time` 30 s, the password turn lock,
  the whole-request deadline, the 8 MiB limit, torqcli's non-dumpable call.
- `tests/snapshots.nix` passes with `--no-new-privs` gone from the owner's half, and with the assertion on
  the subvolume layout gone (the test brings its own layout; `tests/installer.nix:195` checks the
  installed one, which is the real guard).
- `tests/ssh.nix` passes with `MaxAuthTries` 30.
- `cargo test` passes with `prev_hash` not compared at all (`ledger.rs:309`): an entry edited and its own
  hash recomputed is caught by no test. With `verify_entries` gone from `mend_locked` (`ledger.rs:146`),
  so "an edit is never mended" is untested. With `staged.verify()` gone from `check_stage`. With a settling
  entry older than the change accepted (`lib.rs:124`).
- With the agent's 30, 20 and 200 and `MAX_DIFF` all changed together, `cargo test` passes: its tests name
  the constants, not the numbers.
- `tests/strict.nix` passes with break-glass opening the shell after a failed entry (`strict.nix:38`): no
  ledger, no shell, the owner's own rule, has no test. It holds today (M2 ran it).
- **Verified:** mutated, each; `evidence/vm-results.txt`.
- **Fix:** a test each, most a few lines; the table says where. The numbers: one test that asserts the
  literal values, so that changing one is a decision.

### L1 — low — `show system alarms` never names the remote's alarm
- **Where:** `cli/src/status.rs:27-104` reads facts, not `problems`; `broker/src/main.rs:561-583` puts no
  `remote_alarm` among the facts. ADR-036: "the remote in `show system alarms`".
- **Scenario** (run, `evidence/e8-remote.nix`): a ledger cut short here; `push` raises the alarm. `torq-broker
  status` has it. `torqcli show system alarms` says only that the configuration changed outside TorqOS. With
  no other finding it says "a problem this command can't describe (see: torq-broker status)", and as a person
  that command is `Permission denied`; in strict mode sudo will not run it. `show system remote` does have
  it. A rewritten ledger is the one alarm the model most wants seen, and the drift banner is the only
  place a person is told.
- **Fix:** a `remote_alarm` fact in `status --json`, a line in `render`, a test that reads it there; every
  `remote.nix` check today reads `torq-broker status`. The fallback line should name a command the reader can run.

### L2 — low — the share is nosuid only in the agent's namespace
- **Where:** `modules/agent-run.sh:55-58`; ADR-041, 5.
- **What is wrong:** the comment says no set-uid program the agent leaves "works for the person who runs it
  later". The option is on the agent's bind, and the person's `/home` has none. Run: the agent's `chmod 4755`
  in the share stands, `-rwsr-xr-x torq-agent`, on a mount with no `nosuid`, seen from outside. It would run as
  the agent, not as root and less than the person's own: nothing is gained that a share does not give
  (6zg), so low. But it is a way for the agent's code to run outside the launcher's sandbox, and to outlive it.
- **Fix:** correct the comment and the test's name; `torq agent run` could report set-id files in the
  share when it ends. Say in 6zg that `/var/tmp` and `/dev/shm` are shared and outlive a session too (run).

### L3 — low — the fence's reasoning went out of date with the socket
- **Where:** `modules/settings-fence.nix:47-49`; KNOWN-GAPS 6ze ("not sockets or paths (the broker has
  none yet)"). It has one: `systemd.sockets.torq-broker`. Also unfenced: `security.pam.services.torq-approve`.
- **What is wrong:** the fence exists to keep the broker's own inputs out of the tree. Its socket mode, and the
  PAM stack that is the password gate, are now the broker's inputs. Reaching them takes a system change a
  person approves, and the agent's list refuses them unless listed, so this is the fence's own consistency.
- **Verified:** by reading. **Fix:** add both patterns, with `tests/tree` cases; correct 6ze.

### L4 — low — documents that say what is no longer so
Not findings of behaviour. Each is a sentence a reader would rely on.
- SECURITY-MODEL:51, "closed by signing entries with a key root cannot read": ADR-035 says that is not the
  defence. :55, "cannot yet tell a person from a program… KNOWN-GAPS #4, M3". :58-59, "both later" (snapshots
  are built). :39, "`sudo torq-broker` remains the recovery door": not in strict mode. :145, "Kernel lockdown
  (integrity)": see the table. The threat table reads as present tense for things not built.
- KNOWN-GAPS 6y, "reading the history asks for the owner's password": it is anyone's now. #2's "Planned (M3):
  sign the entries". 6zg's "at most 20" (M4), 6zj's full disk (M2), 6zf's `status` (M1), 6zk's "only system
  changes take one… or anyone could push everyone's restore points out" holds, but H1 is a way to a system-tier
  change without a password.
- A2 and 6c: ADR-040 says "A2 comes next on the same branch or the one after", the audit of 11-04 put it in
  M3, and it is not built and not in ROADMAP's M3 list as open. SECURITY-MODEL is honest that it is not
  covered. It should be scheduled or moved, not left. `request system update`'s check is torqcli's, in the
  caller's process: a person with the password who skips torqcli skips it.
- `broker/src/remote.rs:4-7` says `refs/torq/ledger` on the remote; it is the branch `torq-ledger`.
  `tests/remote.nix:142` says fetched "by anyone"; it is people only. CLAUDE.md's current milestone is M2.

## The trust model: what I tried and could not break

Each of these was an attack, not a reading of the design. All by reading the code, unless it says run.

**From the agent's account to root.**
- A diff with `new file mode 120000` or `100755` on a user leaf or a setting: refused both by `check_user_data`
  and by `agent_refusal`, on what the scratch index holds, not what the headers say.
- A rename, a copy, a net-zero section or a second patch of one file to get past the list: `files` is computed from
  the index after the diff, and the agent's base is required and refused when stale, so H1's trick has no second
  HEAD to work from. What the person is shown (`effect`) and what is applied are the same for the agent's.
- Dotted keys, `_type`, type changes at a path (`changed` calls a list or a changed type one setting), `system`
  against `nixos/system.json`, `users/a/b/packages.json`. Nothing got out of the list.
- The nix daemon as the agent: refused (run). `su`, `sudo`, wrappers under `torq agent run`: no new privileges
  (but see M6: no test). `TIOCSTI`: off on this kernel (`legacy_tiocsti` 0), and sudo's pty stands between.
- The ledger's text reaching a root command: `reason` and `files` go to git only as arguments or message lines;
  the label is digits at both ends; `anchor_system` comes only from entries the broker wrote, and only an
  adopted ledger could forge one, and adopt refuses an unsettled commit confirmed.

**From a wheel member to root without an entry, in strict mode.**
- `torq-agent-run` as root: sudo keeps the caller's `PATH`, but every command the script uses is in
  `runtimeInputs`, which come first. The descriptor is checked for owner and name, a swap of the path is caught by
  device and inode, `$fd` is digits before the `eval`, and `$share` is only an argument. Everything the
  person names runs after the drop.
- `torq-break-glass`: `--reason=` in one word, `$SUDO_USER` from sudo, control characters refused, no shell on a
  failure (run: M2's output; no repository test, see the table).
- Every command added after strict mode: `restore-data` runs as the folder's owner and refuses root's; `pull` is a
  candidate; `adopt` refuses a machine with a ledger; `remote-*` are an administrator's. None reaches root by a way
  strict mode assumed shut. A system change still does, ledgered, as 6zj says.
- The list of root's other names in `rootGroups`: I found no group missing that gives root by itself on this base.

**The record.**
- A torn line to hide an entry: a whole entry gets its newline back, anything else is ledgered with its bytes.
  With a remote the cut shows either way (`anchor`), and the mend runs before the push, so a remote never sees a
  torn line. The rewrite by rename holds under the ledger's own lock; break-glass and a restore append under that
  lock, and neither predicts a sequence number that matters (the `Ledger-Entry` trailer is no longer used to check).
- Acknowledging or clearing to wash a rewrite: `remote-clear` forgets `verified`, and the next push walks the whole
  history and compares this ledger with its newest: the alarm comes back while it is true (in `remote.nix`).
  `ledger-acknowledge` is word for word, and a person's. Neither touches what the remote holds.
- Changing `torq remote url` to leave the anchor: the broker read the old URL at start, so the entry saying so
  goes to the old remote first.
- A value that does not round-trip, to make the ledger unreadable for everyone: strings and maps serialise the same
  after parsing, so a caller's text cannot make a line fail the byte-for-byte check.

**Snapshots and restore.**
- A link planted at the target, along the path, or in the snapshot; `..`; a label from a ledger; a stage race in a
  folder of root's (`/home` and `/var/lib` are root's alone, so `chown` by name is safe); a set-id file; a folder
  gone or changed hands during the copy. Root writes nothing into a person's folder. `/var/lib/torq` is out of the
  snapshots on the image and the ISO. I found nothing beyond 6zk.

**The socket.**
- A flag spelt another way, `--`, a value that eats the next argument, a global flag before the command: `check_args`
  and clap agree, and identity flags appended after a `--` make clap fail, not pass. Clap's environment defaults are
  cleared by `serve`. `SO_PEERCRED` with a descriptor handed on needs a process of the victim's to hand it; none does.

**First-boot setup.**
- Before setup the socket is up: no person exists, services propose nothing, `setup`, `adopt` and `adopt-*` are not on
  the socket, a refusal is not ledgered before `init` (mutated, killed), break-glass is refused before the ledger
  begins (mutated, killed). Adopting a repository is trusting it, as 6zi says; nothing gets past that.

**Adopt and pull.**
- A pulled tree's `flake.nix`, lock and hardware file: `from_repo` leaves `lock` and `source` empty, and hardware is
  put back. Unit tests (`session.rs`) read. `adopt`'s checks: a forged `pending_confirm`, a link, a forbidden path, a
  rewritten history, a shown-and-swapped head: each mutated and killed.

## What this audit did not cover

- `installer`, `image`, `adopt` and `trust-loop` were read, not run; adopt's guards were mutated through
  `a_hostile_repository_is_refused_before_anything_is_adopted`, its VM test was not.
- Real rebuilds. H1 and M1 to M4 ran with the rebuild stubbed; H1 on a real machine needs each name to be a real
  package, as it says.
- A real remote: GitHub's latency and rules, a real commit confirmed with a remote.
- The upstreams themselves: sudo, PAM, bubblewrap, setpriv, git's patch parser beyond the behaviour used, the kernel.
- Physical access, the boot chain, the release pipeline since 11-04, aarch64, the desktop.
- `torqcli` beyond its proposal, pull, alarm and launcher paths: its grammar and tree were not re-audited.
- The VM mutants were run three at a time, and under that load the tests flake: three mutants failed in a subtest
  unrelated to them, a timeout or a dropped connection, and were run again alone; only the second result is counted.
  That is itself worth knowing: `strict`, `socket` and `agent-run` have timeouts a loaded host can miss.
